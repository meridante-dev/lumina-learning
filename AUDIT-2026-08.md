### Three uncomfortable readings

**1 · The machine is far ahead of the material.** Twelve admin tabs, eleven
routes, twenty-six evidence event types, a cryptographic proof chain and an
independent verifier — against six placeholder reels, zero registered educators,
and a course in the catalogue with no videos in it. The platform is
enterprise-shaped; the catalogue is a pilot. Every demo currently sells a system
that has little to show inside it.

**2 · The 56% correction rate is simultaneously the best and the most dangerous
number here.** It proves the blind gate is load-bearing rather than decorative —
no competitor can show anything comparable. It also means the generator is wrong
more often than it is right, and **no human has yet signed off a single
question**. Asked "who verified these?", today's honest answer is "a second
model". That is a mechanism, not a governance chain, and for content that credits
legally-mandated hours the difference will matter to somebody's lawyer.

**3 · The primary audience is served worst.** The Land Team works in Portuguese.
Thirty-one modules have English video; seven have Portuguese. Three of four
courses exist in Portuguese only as machine translation.

## Part 2 · The market

### Where the money sits
Cloud LMS runs **$2–15 per user per month**; small buyers pay the upper end.
TalentLMS publishes Core at **$119/mo** and Pro at **$449/mo for 100 users**
(~$4.50/user). Docebo is unpublished, roughly **$7–10 PEPM**, and sized for 250+
learners — it loses under 200 employees to published lower tiers.

At €399/month for a ~30-person team we are ~€13/user — top of the band. The flat
fee is genuinely differentiated for small teams (no per-seat maths, no growth
penalty), but it is a **premium** price and must be defended by something.

### Both of our claimed moats already have incumbents — this matters

**Compliance paperwork is occupied.** Portuguese HR platforms already do the part
buyers ask about first: uMan generates training certificates, attendance lists and
**Relatório Único Annex C** automatically; Factorial centralises training records
against the same obligation; SIGO is the state's own registry.

> **Correction (same day).** An earlier draft of this section claimed "we do not
> generate Annex C at all." That was wrong, and the error came from reading a
> dead code path. `downloadRUannex()` has existed for some time, alongside eleven
> other document exports including the attendance register and several tiers of
> certificate — all already print-based, naming the employer as issuer and the
> content supplier, under a footer that deliberately refuses the word
> *certificado* and cites art. 131.º/3 and art. 133.º. The paperwork layer is
> present and more carefully lawyered than most of this market. What it lacked
> was itemisation on the printed record and brand-grade typography — both now
> fixed. The competitive gap here is narrower than this document first claimed.

**Verifiable credentials are occupied.** BCdiploma, Diplomasafe, VerifyEd and
notably **TrueOriginal — compliance training records, with a REST API for LMS
integration** — all sell tamper-proof credentialing. The digital-credentials
market is ~$1.13bn by 2026 with the blockchain slice growing ~21.7% CAGR. There
is even published work on forging a blockchain credential system, which is the
useful reminder: "blockchain" is not a moat, the *design* is.

### What is genuinely unoccupied
Nobody combines the three. Credential vendors issue certificates but do not
deliver training. HR platforms record hours but cannot prove what happened inside
the lesson. LMSs deliver but their audit trail is immutable only at the vendor's
discretion. Frontline platforms (Axonify, Arist at 84–90% completion via
messaging) win on delivery but sell no evidence layer, and none are built for EU
labour law.

**The unoccupied position: provable frontline training for EU regulated
employers, delivered where the crew already is.** Delivery + proof + paperwork, in
Portuguese, on WhatsApp. Each leg exists in the market alone; the combination does
not.

## Part 3 · What leading requires

Ranked by what actually blocks the claim.

**1 · Put a human in the verification chain.** One reviewer approving each
question, recorded with who and when, surfaced in the Checks tab beside the
model verdicts. Without it the 56% is an exposure; with it, "every question was
written from the transcript, machine-audited, and signed off by a named person"
is a sentence no competitor can say. *Small build, real human hours.*

**2 · Generate Relatório Único Annex C, certificates and attendance registers.**
This is the buying question in Portugal and a competitor already answers it. We
hold better raw data than any of them — we just do not emit the form. *Medium.*

**3 · Fill the catalogue or narrow the pitch.** A course with zero videos, six
placeholder reels and no educators cannot lead anything. Either produce (teach-back
is the cheapest route) or stop demoing empty surfaces.

**4 · Portuguese-native content for every course**, not translation. The audience
is Portuguese; the evidence claim is that records say what they mean.

**5 · xAPI out, SCORM in.** Procurement checklists and migration paths. Neither is
interesting; both end deals.

**6 · The passport as a European Digital Credential.** With 1–2 done, this
becomes the flagship: an EU-recognised credential carrying an evidence reference
and a verification method the issuer cannot forge, for a worker who keeps it.

**7 · WhatsApp delivery as a first-class channel**, not a side door. Arist's
numbers say the channel is worth more than the portal.

### Sequence
**1 → 2 → 3** is the leadership-credibility block and should happen before any
new surface is built. **5** when a deal needs it. **4** continuously. **6 and 7**
are the second half.

### The strategic warning
The build has been outrunning the material and the governance. Twelve admin tabs
and an anchored proof chain do not win a deal that is lost on "can you produce
Annex C?" and "who checked these questions?" Both answers are cheap to fix and
both are currently no.
